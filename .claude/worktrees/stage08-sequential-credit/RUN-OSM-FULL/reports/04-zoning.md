## 2. The partition

SPEC §4 Stage B is explicit that this is a **graph** partition, not a geometric one:
the Mtkvari and the ridgelines mean two points 500 m apart can be 6 km apart by
road. So the partition runs over `L(G)`'s own adjacency, and a zone is connected if
you can drive across it, not if it looks compact on a map.

The unit is a **segment** — one physical street piece carrying one or two directed
arcs — rather than an arc. Both directions of a street are driven on the same day by
anyone sane, and splitting them across zones would manufacture deadhead that no
route solver could recover.

| --- | ---: |

| Longest day, before → after | 9.1 → 8.5 h |
| **Day duration spread, as driven** | **0.002 h** (gate: ≤ 1.0 h) |

**Balancing capture time is not the same as balancing days.** A far zone with the
same capture time is a longer day, because two depot legs sit inside the shift
(SPEC §5). METIS balances whatever it is handed, so it is handed capture time *plus
each segment's share of the leg its zone will pay* — which is SPEC §7.1's third
mitigation, "zone sizing on net productive time", expressed in the only language a
partitioner speaks. The result is that far zones automatically carry less work.

Contiguity is required of METIS directly rather than repaired afterwards, and the
local search that finishes the balance never moves a segment that is an articulation
point of its zone — so no day is ever in two pieces.

## 4. Capture on the commute

SPEC §7.1's first mitigation: a depot leg is only deadhead if the corridor it runs
along is already covered. Where it is not, the leg drives uncovered required arcs at
capture speed instead of passing them at deadhead speed, and that work stops being
owed by a zone.

The accounting is deliberately conservative — the leg gains
`capture(e) − deadhead(e)` and the zone loses `capture(e)`, and **not** the deadhead
the zone would have spent reaching it. Crediting that too would produce a bigger
number with nothing behind it, so the figures below are a floor.

That accounting also makes the routing exact rather than heuristic: under it the net
cost of driving an uncovered required arc is **zero**, so the best coverage-aware leg
is an ordinary shortest path with those arcs free. One Dijkstra, no orienteering
problem.

| Window | Days | Days with a productive leg | Arcs captured | Capture reclaimed | Campaign hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10h | 8 | 0 / 8 | 0 | 0.0 | 75.3 |
| 9h | 9 | 0 / 9 | 0 | 0.0 | 76.3 |

**It works, and it is smaller than SPEC §7.1 hoped.** The spec expects this to
"reclaim most of the two hours" on eastern days. It reclaims
0.0 h across the whole 9h campaign.
The reason is §3: once routed, the legs are not two hours. There is less to reclaim
than the spec assumed because the problem is smaller than the spec assumed.

The binding constraint is not the detour — it is the **day budget**. A zone sized to
fill its shift has no room for a leg that takes longer than the plain one, so the
productive leg gets refused. `config.COMMUTE_ALLOWANCE` (currently 0.00) reserves room in proportion to each zone's
leg, which is why the far zones — first in the sequence, when least is covered —
reserve the most.

**The trade-off, measured.** Raising the allowance to 0.20 or above roughly triples
the reclaim and drives about 9 hours less in total — but needs one more day. SPEC §4
names total driving hours as the KPI and by that measure the larger allowance wins;
the calendar is what `DECISION-schedule.md` actually turned on, so the default keeps
the day count down. It is one constant, and the number to weigh it against is here
rather than buried in the code.

## 5. Sequence — far zones first

SPEC §7.1 mitigation 2, and the spec calls it a hard rule: **distant zones are driven
first**. Early in the campaign the connecting corridors are still uncovered, so a
long leg absorbs productive work; late in the campaign the same corridors are done
and the same leg is pure deadhead. The spec also says this is counter-intuitive and
easy to implement backwards, so the gate asserts the ordering rather than trusting
it, and the map colours by sequence position so a reversed rule would be visible.

partitioning failure.

## 6. Daylight

SPEC §7.3 was rewritten on Stage 3 evidence: daylight *is* binding. Each day's window
is capped at `min(shift_end, sunset)`, with sunset computed from the NOAA solar
position algorithm for the middle of the municipality.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10h | 8 | 19:41 | 19:30 | 0.0 | 2027-03-17 | 200 |
| 9h | 9 | 19:41 | 19:28 | 0.0 | 2027-03-17 | 200 |

**No day in this plan is daylight-limited.** Starting 2026-08-29 the campaign finishes
2026-09-06, when the sun still sets at
19:28 — 88 minutes after the shift
ends. The taper costs 0.0 hours.

That is a statement about *this* start date, not about the plan. The number that
matters is the one underneath it:

> **The decision can wait until 2027-03-17** —
> 200 days from the modelled start. One day
> later and the plan no longer fits its own windows.

`DECISION-schedule.md` put that date at 28 September, from Stage 3 day counts and a
flat 1.2 h/day depot-leg allowance. Routed legs and a real partition move it to
**2027-03-17**. The direction of the correction is
favourable, and it is the kind of thing worth knowing before committing a vehicle.

The 9 h window tolerates a *later* start than the 10 h one despite needing more
days, which looks wrong and is not. Far-zones-first sorts the long days to the front
of the campaign, so the days that need the most daylight are driven while there is
most of it, and the short near-depot days are the ones left to run into November.
SPEC §7.1 justifies the rule on coverage grounds alone; it turns out to buy schedule
robustness as well.

### 6.1 A caveat the model does not carry

The window is capped at sunset, which is the letter of the brief. SPEC §7.3 also
warns that "the final hour degrades before it disappears" — low sun, long shadows,
glare. Stereo capture quality in the last half hour before sunset is an **accuracy**
question under SPEC §1.1, not a scheduling one, and nothing in this pipeline measures
it. If a later start pushes days into late October, that margin should be taken off
the window explicitly rather than discovered at footage review.
