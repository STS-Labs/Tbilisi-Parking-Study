## 1. Variant D, measured

**Why this was re-run.** Variant D was ruled out on 25 August because it "needed to
start driving by 6 September", a date derived from a flat **1.2 h/day** depot-leg
allowance applied to Stage 3's totals. Stage 4 then routed the legs over `L(G)` and
measured **18.4 h across the entire
variant C campaign** — about 33 minutes a day, not 1.2 hours. The arithmetic that
ruled D out was built on an allowance more than twice the real figure.

So D was measured, not scaled. Its requirements are more numerous and more
scattered than C's, so its legs, its balance and its zone count all had to be
computed — extrapolating from C is the error that made this re-check necessary in
the first place, and repeating it here would have been worse than not checking.

Margin is against the 2026-10-30 daylight limit — the last date a
shift ending at 18:00 is fully daylit, from Stage 4's solar model. **Every row
fits.** Starting 25 August, no day in any of the four plans is daylight-limited, so
the taper column is zero throughout.

**The last two columns need reading together.** "Latest start" applies the daylight
rule literally — a day fits if it ends before sunset — and a late start survives it
because far-zones-first puts the *short* days at the end, where the short days are.
But the finish dates those starts imply run deep into November, and SPEC §7.3 warns
that "low winter sun produces long shadows and glare". That is a capture-**accuracy**
cost under SPEC §1.1, and nothing in this pipeline measures it. Treat the latest-start
column as the point where the schedule breaks, not as a date worth planning to.

## 2. Disposing of the trapped mainlines

Under variant C, 12.2 km of required motorway and
trunk cannot appear in any closed tour — 6.4%
of the 190.0 km fast network. SPEC §1.1 requires a
disposition, and the first question is whether the cause is a modelling rule or the
road network.

Stage 1 already has the machinery: rebuild `L(G)` with one rule dropped at a time
and see what comes back. That is reused here rather than reimplemented.

| Cause | Arcs | km | Motorway/trunk arcs | Motorway/trunk km |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `mainline_entry_rule` | 30 | 5.2 | 23 | 4.5 |
| `clip_buffer_edge` | 16 | 4.6 | 16 | 4.6 |
| `base_topology` | 12 | 2.8 | 12 | 2.8 |
| `u_turn_policy` | 48 | 2.6 | 0 | 0.0 |
| `turn_restrictions` | 35 | 1.5 | 5 | 0.3 |

Three things fall out of that table.

**The U-turn policy strands no fast road at all.** It accounts for 2.6 km of trapped
arcs, and *none* of it is motorway or trunk — which is what SPEC §3.3 intends, since
those classes carry no U-turn edges by design. The brief raised it as a candidate
cause; it is ruled out, and the instruction not to widen the policy globally is moot.

**Clip-boundary geometry accounts for 4.6 km.** These are arcs at or
beyond the edge of the extract, already classified in Stage 0b. They are an artefact
of where the download stops, not a gap in Tbilisi's coverage.

**Base topology accounts for 2.8 km.** Genuinely disconnected
in the source data. No rule change reaches it; it is a data-quality finding and
belongs in the same bucket as Stage 0's hillside fragments.

That leaves the mainline-entry rule.

### 2.1 The narrowest fix

SPEC §3.3 makes a mainline enterable only from a ramp. Where OSM models **no ramp at
a junction**, there is no ramp to insist on: the plain junction is what the data says
the access is, and refusing it invents a restriction that is not there on the ground.

The proposed narrowing keeps the rule at every junction that has a ramp and relaxes
it only at junctions that have none. It is one condition, and this is its whole
effect:

| Measure | Rule as built | Rule narrowed | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manoeuvres in `L(G)` | 119,847 | 119,850 | +3 |
| Arcs a closed tour can reach | 80,347 | 80,401 | +54 |
| Trapped requirement | 16.7 km | 10.6 km | −6.1 km |
| …of it motorway/trunk | 12.2 km | 7.5 km | −4.7 km |

**3 manoeuvres recover 4.7 km of
required motorway and trunk.** All three are the same shape — a trunk road meeting a
motorway at a junction OSM models without a slip road, which in Tbilisi is exactly
the case Stage 1 flagged when it noted that trunk here is an at-grade arterial class:

| From | Class | To | Class | OSM node |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `384788655:0:B` | trunk | `1225044771:0:F` | motorway | 221,397,588 |
| `75381841:0:F` | trunk | `659652289:0:F` | motorway | 220,807,230 |
| `80322345:0:F` | trunk | `26453630:1:F` | motorway | 937,476,350 |

**Invariant U1 holds.** Narrowing a rule only *adds* edges, and everything else is
the graph the Stage 1 gate already cleared — so the check here is not another random
sample but an **exhaustive** one over the 3 additions, against the
1,016 manoeuvres the parsed OSM relations forbid.
Violations: **0**. A junction OSM models as a junction is
drivable, and no turn restriction says otherwise at any of the three.

**What adopting it costs.** `L(G)` changes, so Stage 1's graph, Stage 3's bounds and
Stage 4's zoning all have to be rebuilt — about forty minutes of compute and a
re-run of four gates. Nothing else. The flag is
`config.MAINLINE_ENTRY_FALLBACK_WHERE_NO_RAMP`, and it is **off**: this is a
proposal with a measurement attached, not a change made on the way past. If the
scope decision in §5 moves the project to variant D, Stages 1–4 are being rebuilt
anyway and adopting it then costs nothing extra.

## 3. The extra day

Stage 4 reserved no room for capture on the commute and took the fewest days,
because `DECISION-schedule.md` had turned on the calendar. Stage 4 then measured the
slack and found 42 days of it, so that constraint is gone. SPEC §4 states plainly
that **total driving hours is the KPI**.

On variant C the reserved profile costs one day and saves
8.0 h of driving; on D it costs one day and saves
5.9 h. The direction is the same on both, and the brief's
instruction is unambiguous: **take the day**.

`config.COMMUTE_ALLOWANCE` is now `0.35` and
`COMMUTE_ALLOWANCE_RESERVED` is `0.35`; both profiles
are reported every run so the trade-off never has to be reconstructed from memory.

One honest caveat: reserving the room **widens the day spread**, from
0.566 h to
0.962 h on C. Capture-on-the-commute takes
work off the early far zones, so the days it helps most end up shortest. It is a
real effect and it eats into the balance headroom the Stage 4 gate measures.

## 4. What the decision now turns on

`DECISION-schedule.md` chose C over D for schedule certainty, and recorded that this
was the one place SPEC §1.1 was overridden. The measurement that forced the choice
has since been replaced by a better one, and the choice does not survive it:

**The day count was never the problem.** The decision estimated 55.4 days for D and
the measured figure is 54 — close enough that scaling was not the error.
The error was the *start-by* date: it was derived as "1 November minus the day
count", which assumes the campaign must both start and finish inside the daylight
window as a block. It does not. Far-zones-first puts the long days first, so a
campaign can start later than that subtraction allows and still close every day
before sunset. That is what moved 6 September to 2026-09-30.

|  | As decided, 25 Aug | As measured, Stage 4b |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Depot-leg allowance | 1.2 h/day (assumed) | 33 min/day (routed) |
| Variant D, days at 10 h | 55.4 (estimated) | 54 (measured) |
| Variant D, must start driving by | 6 September | 2026-09-30 (finishing 2026-11-22) |
| Variant D finishes | past the limit | 2026-10-17 (+13 d) |
| 1,150 km of through service roads | deferred to phase 2 | **recoverable now** |

**The recommendation is to move to variant D.** It restores the 1,150 km that SPEC
§2.1.1 warned would cost *more* to sweep up later than to drive now, and it reverses
the project's only §1.1 override. What it costs is 21 more days of driving and
about forty minutes of recompute.

The margin is not the same at both windows: **+13 days at 10 h** but only
**+5 at 9 h**. If the shift decision
lands on the 9 h window, D is still inside the limit but has under a week of slack,
and any slippage in Stage 5 eats it. The two decisions are coupled and should not be
taken in ignorance of each other.

Three things should be decided together rather than in sequence, because each is
cheap only while the others are being done:

1. **Scope** — D rather than C, on the numbers in §1.
2. **The mainline-entry narrowing** (§2), which needs a rebuild of Stages 1–4 that a
   scope change is already paying for.
3. **The reserved commute allowance** (§3), which is already the default and will be
   re-derived on D's own numbers by that rebuild.

**Stage 5 should not start until this is settled.** It consumes the zones, and all
three of the above change them.

What remains undecided either way is the residual unreachable requirement — 57.5 km under D, of which the clip-edge and
base-topology parts are not fixable by any rule. SPEC §1.1 still wants a disposition
on those before Stage 5, and this stage does not provide one: they are reported, not
resolved.
