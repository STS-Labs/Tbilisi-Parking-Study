## The headline number

> ## Required traversal: **674.4 km**
>
> Of which **652.4 km** lies inside the
> largest strongly connected component and is therefore actually drivable as one tour.

Required traversal km = Σ (`length_m` × required passes), counting each narrow
two-way pair once. It is the quantity that sets the campaign length: at the
40 km/h capture cap of SPEC §5 it is the numerator of every schedule estimate.

### 8.1 By `service=*` subtag

`highway=service` is a bag of unrelated things: an alley is a street the campaign has
to capture, a driveway or a parking aisle is not. The scope decision cannot be made on
the parent tag, so here is the subtag split.

> **The subtag does not settle it.** 89.5% of service required traversal — 253.6 km of

## Findings, and what the next stages should do with them

**1. The `min_passes = 2` fallback does not fire often enough to need calibrating.**
SPEC §2.1 says that if a large number of arcs reach the default, the lane threshold gets
calibrated against imagery before Stage 4. The measured count is 21 arcs (1.1 km, 0.03% of included arcs) — Tbilisi's major roads
carry `lanes` often enough that the chain almost always resolves before the default.
**That trigger does not fire.** No imagery calibration is needed on this account.

**2. Two same-direction passes are a small effect here — smaller than SPEC §2.2 assumes.**
SPEC §2.2 calls repeat passes "the most important cost consequence in the spec", and the
open [CONFIRM] asks whether a 6-lane one-way needs three passes. Measured: only
397 arcs carry `min_passes = 2`, contributing 33.6 km — 5.0% of the headline.
Raising the threshold to 3 passes on the widest arcs can therefore move the campaign by
at most a fraction of that. **This [CONFIRM] is low-stakes and can be resolved cheaply.**

**3. `highway=service` is the expensive open decision — 42.0% of the headline.**
Including service roads adds 283.4 km of required traversal (35,825 arcs).
SPEC §1.1 makes *include* the default and that is how the network is built, but this is
the single largest number attached to any unresolved question in the spec — an order of
magnitude more than the lane-threshold questions. **Resolve this one first.**

**4. The narrow-pair rule, not the lane threshold, is the real modelling lever.**
13,546 segments (425.3 km) are treated as narrow two-way pairs, so a single traversal
satisfies both directions. 358.7 km of that is decided by **class alone** — no `lanes`,
no `width`, just `residential`/`service`/`unclassified`/`living_street`. If that assumption is
wrong, those streets need both directions and the headline rises by up to 358.7 km —
roughly 53.2% of the campaign. **This is the assumption worth spending imagery on**, not
the `lanes > 3` threshold. SPEC §2.1 does not currently name a width limit for it; one should
be agreed before Stage 3 fixes the lower bound.

**5. Tagging is sparse enough that Stage 2's time model will be class-driven.**
`maxspeed` is present on 14.3% of included arcs and `width` on 3.2%. SPEC §6 allows for
`maxspeed` to override free-flow speed by class; in practice the class table will set the speed
for roughly 86% of the network, so that table deserves the attention a per-arc override
would otherwise get.

**6. 40.7 km reads steeper than the drivability limit and was deliberately kept.**
1,600 included arcs have a DEM gradient beyond ±20%, of which 11 (0.2 km)
sit on a bridge or in a tunnel, where SRTM is measuring the valley floor rather than the
road. The rest are a mix of genuine Tbilisi hillside streets and DEM error at cuttings and
embankments. None were excluded (§1.1). **Stage 2 must not feed these straight into a speed
model** — a bridge deck at a false −40% would be modelled as a 12 km/h crawl.

**7. The orphan fragments are mostly gated yards, not a filter bug.**
The largest SCC holds 96.0% of included arc km, comfortably past the 95% gate.
The 355 fragments total 97.1 km, of which 76.6 km is `highway=service`.
That is the expected shape: courtyards and parking aisles reachable only through a way
the drivability filter removed. If `highway=service` is dropped per finding 3, most of
these fragments leave with it.

**8. The barrier filter is deliberately coarse and slightly over-excludes.**
A blocking barrier node removes the whole segment it sits inside (1,125 arcs, 56.1 km),
rather than the portion beyond it. Splitting at barrier nodes would recover part of that
km; it belongs with Stage 1's turn expansion, which has to look at those nodes anyway.

**9. One-way data looks sound.** 77 segments are one-way by implication rather than by tag, and
only 8 link segments (0.5 km) are modelled two-way — ramps almost never are,
so a large number here would have meant a tagging gap. It is not large. SPEC §2.2's couplet
mitigation has real material to work with: one-way streets are 16.0% of centerline km.

**10. The DEM is SRTM, not Copernicus GLO-30, and not by preference.**
GLO-30 publishes no tiles over the N41/E044 and N41/E045 cells — they are withheld from
the free instance for this border region. SRTM 1-arcsec is the stage brief's named
alternative at the same 30 m ground resolution, so the substitution costs nothing in
resolution, but it is a source difference worth recording.
