## 1. `service_role` — carrying the service decision forward

SPEC §2.1 leaves `highway=service` open and Stage 0b closed the tag route: 92.2% of
service arcs carry no `service=*` value at all, so the subtag cannot decide anything
for the bulk of 2,193 km. **Connectivity can.** Collapse the service-only arcs into
undirected components and ask each component how many *distinct* non-service nodes
it touches.

| `service_role` | Rule | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `through` | component touches ≥ 2 | carries traffic between streets — functionally a street |
| `spur` | exactly 1 | dead-end tree: driveway, yard, parking aisle |
| `orphan` | 0 | unreachable from the street network |

Both sides of the test use **included** arcs only. An `access=private` or barriered
arc is not a door the camera car can drive through, so counting it as one would
promote gated yards to through routes — it moves 45.5 km out of `orphan` on evidence
the car cannot use. That is the §1.1-consistent reading: connectivity you cannot
drive is not connectivity.

## 2. The speed model

Every term is a multiplicative factor on speed:

```
v          = v_base(class | maxspeed) · f_gradient · f_surface · f_width / c_congestion
v_capture  = v                 where capture_quality == "reduced"   (SPEC §3.2)
           = min(v, 40 km/h)   everywhere else                      (SPEC §5)
t_capture  = length_m / (v_capture / 3.6)
```

Multiplicative means **linear in log speed**, and that is the design, not a
convenience: it is what lets Stage 6 refit every coefficient from map-matched traces
with one ordinary least-squares solve. See §2.8.

### 2.3 Gradient — and how much of it is the DEM talking

`f_gradient = exp(−k·g)`, with `k = 0.07636` uphill and
half that downhill.

**The uphill coefficient is not chosen — it is solved.** SPEC §6 states that "a 12%
Tbilisi lane is 12 km/h, not 40". A residential lane free-flows at 30 km/h, so a 12%
grade costs it a factor of 12/30, and `k = −ln(0.4)/12`. One anchor fixes the whole
term, which means there is one gradient assumption to argue with rather than four.
The exponential form rather than `1/(1+k·g)` is what keeps the model log-linear.

**The deadband is measured.** Stage 0 §10 warns that a 30 m DEM samples terrain, not
road deck. That warning can be quantified: bridge and tunnel arcs of 25 m or less are
flat by construction, so whatever elevation change the DEM reports across them is
close to pure sampling error. Over 1,426 such arcs the median |Δz| is **0.52 m** —
which on the median 39 m arc is a spurious 1.3% grade, and on the shortest arcs is
4%. The correction therefore subtracts a fixed *elevation* error, and so scales with
1/length, rather than subtracting a fixed gradient.

| Constant | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GRADIENT_ANCHOR_PCT` / `_FACTOR` | 12.0% → 0.400 | SPEC §6, verbatim |
| `GRADIENT_UPHILL_DECAY_PER_PCT` | 0.07636 | derived: `−ln(0.4)/12` |
| `GRADIENT_DOWNHILL_DECAY_RATIO` | 0.5 | **assumption** — descending costs less than climbing, but not nothing |
| `GRADIENT_DEM_NOISE_M` | 0.50 m | **measured** — median \|Δz\| on ≤ 25 m bridge/tunnel decks |
| `GRADIENT_DEADBAND_MAX_PCT` | 5.0% | a 5 m arc would otherwise lose a real grade to a 10% deadband |
| `GRADIENT_CAP_PCT` | 12.0% | beyond this a 30 m DEM is not evidence (Stage 0 §10); penalty saturates at 0.40 |
| `structure` in {bridge, tunnel} | gradient → 0 | SPEC §6 — a DEM reads terrain, not a deck |

**What the corrections do to the distribution and to the hours**

| Measure | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Raw \|gradient\| p50 / p90 | 4.12% / 12.62% |
| Effective \|gradient\| p50 / p90 | 1.93% / 10.37% |
| Arcs zeroed by the deadband | 29.1% |
| Arcs at the 12% cap | 7.1% |
| Flat-structure arcs | 1,258 |
| Capture hours, gradient term **off** | 25.9 |
| Capture hours, **no** deadband | 33.0 |
| **Capture hours as modelled** | **31.2** |

The gradient term adds **5.2 h** — 20.2%
of the free-flow capture total, so the stage brief is right that it is material
city-wide. Of the raw DEM signal, **1.8 h** was measurement error the
deadband removed: roughly a fifth of the term. Without that correction the model
would be fitting SRTM's vertical error on 39-metre arcs and calling it Tbilisi's
hills.

### 2.8 Fittability — the Stage 6 interface, written now

SPEC §10 map-matches each day's trace back onto these arcs. `SpeedModel.fit` is the
entry point, and it exists because of how the model is shaped rather than as a stub:

```
log v = Σ β_class·1[class] + β_up·g⁺ + β_down·g⁻ + Σ β_surface·1[surface]
        + β_narrow·narrow + log(maxspeed)
```

Every coefficient is a single `numpy.linalg.lstsq` away. Three details make it work
on real traces rather than on paper:

- **Congestion is divided back out.** An observation is congested; `fit` takes the
  index the model believed at the time and recovers free flow, so a rush-hour trace
  does not permanently slow the class table.
- **A posted `maxspeed` enters as an offset, not a class column.** Posted-speed arcs
  then constrain the penalty terms without biasing the class table that only applies
  where nothing is posted.
- **Coefficients with no observations keep their prior.** A partial campaign refits
  only what it drove.

The coefficients live in `02-time-model.json`, and `pipeline.rebuild_arc_table` reads
them back. A refit therefore propagates to every downstream stage by re-running
Stage 2 — no code change, and the determinism gate covers the new numbers.

## 3. Congestion — the assumed part

**There is no traffic data for Tbilisi in this pipeline.** Every number in this
section is a declared assumption. What follows is not offered as a measurement, and
the sensitivity table in §4 exists precisely so that no one has to take it as one.

The index is a **travel-time ratio**: 1.00 is free flow, 1.45 means the journey takes
45% longer. Classes are grouped by exposure to the flows that congest — arterials
carry through traffic and clog worst, while residential and service streets are
obstructed by parking and manoeuvring, which does not follow the peak.

Time-of-day bands: `am_peak` 9:00–10:00, `midday` 10:00–16:00, `pm_peak` 16:00–18:00.

Congestion is applied **before** the 40 km/h cap, not after, and that ordering is
the whole story of §4: a car cannot capture faster than the traffic in front of it,
and it will not capture faster than 40 regardless. Where traffic is already slower
than 40 the cap never binds and congestion bites in full; where free flow exceeds 40
the cap absorbs the traffic entirely. Congestion can only move the answer in the band
between the two, and §2.6 measured that band at
12.4%
of required kilometres.

The sweep scales the assumed **delay**, not the ratio: `index(λ) = 1 + λ·(index − 1)`.
Scaling the ratio itself would make "+50% congestion" mean a 2.2× travel time on a
road that is barely congested, which is not what the phrase means to a scheduler. The
depot legs move with λ too (§5.1), since on eastern days they are a fifth of the
shift and holding them fixed would understate exactly the risk the table is for.

### 5.1 Depot legs

### 5.3 What this estimate excludes

**Deadhead.** Repositioning between required arcs is not counted anywhere above.
SPEC §4 Stage A is the first thing in this project entitled to say what the deadhead
ratio is, and putting a placeholder here would give the reader a number no
measurement stands behind. The estimate is therefore a **floor**.

What can be said without asserting a value is the slope:

| Deadhead ratio | Days (variant A) | Whole days |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0.0% | 7.6 | 8 |
| 10.0% | 8.4 | 9 |
| 20.0% | 9.2 | 10 |

These are **illustrations, not estimates**. SPEC §4's KPI warning also applies: the
hybrid coverage rule deliberately raises the deadhead ratio, so a high ratio here
would not by itself be a failure — total driving hours is the KPI.
