## The headline number

> ## Required traversal: **5,725.3 km**
>
> Of which **5,607.6 km** lies inside the
> largest strongly connected component and is therefore actually drivable as one tour.

Required traversal km = Σ (`length_m` × required passes), counting each narrow
two-way pair once. It is the quantity that sets the campaign length: at the
40 km/h capture cap of SPEC §5 it is the numerator of every schedule estimate.

## 8. `highway=service` — the open scope decision (SPEC §2.1)

| Measure | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Arcs | 95,214 |
| Included arcs | 88,877 |
| Included centerline km | 2,193.7 |
| **Required traversal km** | **2,196.0** |
| Share of the headline number | 38.4% |

SPEC §2.1 leaves this open and §1.1 makes *include* the default, so service roads are
**in** the network as built. The number above is the cost of that decision: dropping
`highway=service` would remove exactly this much required traversal.


### 8.1 By `service=*` subtag

`highway=service` is a bag of unrelated things: an alley is a street the campaign has
to capture, a driveway or a parking aisle is not. The scope decision cannot be made on
the parent tag, so here is the subtag split.

| `service_type` | Arcs | Included arcs | Centerline km | Required km | % of service |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `untagged` | 86,248 | 80,336 | 2,196.9 | 2,025.3 | 92.2% |
| `driveway` | 6,378 | 6,065 | 119.4 | 113.4 | 5.2% |
| `parking_aisle` | 1,751 | 1,683 | 40.4 | 38.4 | 1.7% |
| `alley` | 750 | 708 | 16.1 | 16.6 | 0.8% |
| `drive-through` | 57 | 57 | 1.9 | 1.9 | 0.1% |
| `driving_learning` | 18 | 18 | 0.3 | 0.3 | 0.0% |
| `emergency_access` | 10 | 8 | 0.2 | 0.1 | 0.0% |
| `ramp` | 2 | 2 | 0.1 | 0.1 | 0.0% |

> **The subtag does not settle it.** 92.2% of service required traversal — 2,025.3 km of
> 2,196.0 km — carries **no `service=*` tag at all**. The values that would let the
> question be answered on tags (`driveway`, `parking_aisle`) cover only 170.7 km
> between them, 7.8% of the service total and 3.0% of the campaign.
>
> So the open [CONFIRM] in SPEC §2.1 stays open on this evidence. Deciding it needs a
> sample of what those untagged service ways physically *are*, not more tag analysis.


### 8.2 Scope scenarios — measurement only

Each variant is rebuilt properly rather than subtracted: dropping service roads changes
which arcs remain strongly connected, so the component analysis and L(G) are recomputed on
the filtered network. **Variant A is what the pipeline builds and remains the default** —
no switch is exposed and no classification changed.

| Variant | What it includes | Required km | Δ vs A | Included arcs | Δ vs A | L(G) nodes | Δ vs A |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | as built — all `highway=service` | 5,725.3 | +0.0 | 170,176 | +0 | 166,693 | +0 |
| **B** | streets only — service where `service_type` is alley or untagged | 5,571.2 | -154.1 | 162,343 | -7,833 | 158,578 | -8,115 |
| **C** | none — no `highway=service` | 3,529.4 | -2,196.0 | 81,299 | -88,877 | 80,619 | -86,074 |

**Variant B saves almost nothing** — 154.1 km, 2.7% of the campaign — because
the untagged bucket dominates and B keeps it. The real choice is between A and C, and
C is a 2,196.0 km cut made without knowing what is being cut.


### 8.3 What the untagged service ways actually are — a 12-sample read

§8.1 leaves 2,025.3 km undecided because it carries no subtag. Tags cannot resolve that,
so this is a small imagery sample of the population itself. **It is an indication, not a
measurement** — see the caveat below.

| Category | Samples | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `street_like` | 4 | the only vehicle access to dwellings along it — a camera car would treat it as a street |
| `yard` | 4 | industrial, agricultural or commercial service and loading area |
| `parking` | 4 | car-park aisle or courtyard parking |

12 segments drawn from the 40,888 included untagged-service segments, weighted by length so the sample represents km rather than count; numpy default_rng seed 20260825. Reviewed on Esri World Imagery at z19.

> **n=12. This is an indication of the split, NOT a measurement: the standard error on a one-third proportion at n=12 is about 14 percentage points. A defensible estimate needs on the order of 100 samples.**

A second, full-population check points the same way without settling it: only **5.4%** of
untagged-service required km carries a street `name`, against **72.7%** for `residential`.
That is consistent with most of it not being street-like, but it cannot be read as a
measurement either — of the four street-like samples above, only one was named.

**What this means for the decision.** Neither variant A nor variant C is clearly right.
If the split is near a third, then roughly 675 km of the 2,025 km is street the campaign
should capture and roughly 1,350 km is yards and parking aisles it should not — so A
over-drives by about 1,350 km and C under-covers by about 675 km. Both are large enough
to matter and the sample is too small to choose between them. The cheap next step is the
same sampling at n≈100, which would narrow the interval to a few percentage points; the
expensive one is driving either error.

## Findings, and what the next stages should do with them

**1. The `min_passes = 2` fallback does not fire often enough to need calibrating.**
SPEC §2.1 says that if a large number of arcs reach the default, the lane threshold gets
calibrated against imagery before Stage 4. The measured count is 147 arcs (8.1 km, 0.09% of included arcs) — Tbilisi's major roads
carry `lanes` often enough that the chain almost always resolves before the default.
**That trigger does not fire.** No imagery calibration is needed on this account.

**2. Two same-direction passes are a small effect here — smaller than SPEC §2.2 assumes.**
SPEC §2.2 calls repeat passes "the most important cost consequence in the spec", and the
open [CONFIRM] asks whether a 6-lane one-way needs three passes. Measured: only
977 arcs carry `min_passes = 2`, contributing 95.1 km — 1.7% of the headline.
Raising the threshold to 3 passes on the widest arcs can therefore move the campaign by
at most a fraction of that. **This [CONFIRM] is low-stakes and can be resolved cheaply.**

**3. `highway=service` is the expensive open decision — 38.4% of the headline.**
Including service roads adds 2,196.0 km of required traversal (88,877 arcs).
SPEC §1.1 makes *include* the default and that is how the network is built, but this is
the single largest number attached to any unresolved question in the spec — an order of
magnitude more than the lane-threshold questions. **Resolve this one first.**

**4. The narrow-pair rule, not the lane threshold, is the real modelling lever.**
74,374 segments (4,526.9 km) are treated as narrow two-way pairs, so a single traversal
satisfies both directions. 4,095.4 km of that is decided by **class alone** — no `lanes`,
no `width`, just `residential`/`service`/`unclassified`/`living_street`. If that assumption is
wrong, those streets need both directions and the headline rises by up to 4,095.4 km —
roughly 71.5% of the campaign. **This is the assumption worth spending imagery on**, not
the `lanes > 3` threshold. SPEC §2.1 does not currently name a width limit for it; one should
be agreed before Stage 3 fixes the lower bound.

**5. Tagging is sparse enough that Stage 2's time model will be class-driven.**
`maxspeed` is present on 9.5% of included arcs and `width` on 1.6%. SPEC §6 allows for
`maxspeed` to override free-flow speed by class; in practice the class table will set the speed
for roughly 91% of the network, so that table deserves the attention a per-arc override
would otherwise get.

**6. 60.4 km reads steeper than the drivability limit and was deliberately kept.**
2,145 included arcs have a DEM gradient beyond ±20%, of which 11 (0.2 km)
sit on a bridge or in a tunnel, where SRTM is measuring the valley floor rather than the
road. The rest are a mix of genuine Tbilisi hillside streets and DEM error at cuttings and
embankments. None were excluded (§1.1). **Stage 2 must not feed these straight into a speed
model** — a bridge deck at a false −40% would be modelled as a 12 km/h crawl.

**7. The orphan fragments are mostly gated yards, not a filter bug.**
The largest SCC holds 97.9% of included arc km, comfortably past the 95% gate.
The 449 fragments total 193.1 km, of which 144.0 km is `highway=service`.
That is the expected shape: courtyards and parking aisles reachable only through a way
the drivability filter removed. If `highway=service` is dropped per finding 3, most of
these fragments leave with it.

**8. The barrier filter is deliberately coarse and slightly over-excludes.**
A blocking barrier node removes the whole segment it sits inside (1,344 arcs, 71.1 km),
rather than the portion beyond it. Splitting at barrier nodes would recover part of that
km; it belongs with Stage 1's turn expansion, which has to look at those nodes anyway.

**9. One-way data looks sound.** 137 segments are one-way by implication rather than by tag, and
only 32 link segments (1.1 km) are modelled two-way — ramps almost never are,
so a large number here would have meant a tagging gap. It is not large. SPEC §2.2's couplet
mitigation has real material to work with: one-way streets are 9.3% of centerline km.

**10. The DEM is SRTM, not Copernicus GLO-30, and not by preference.**
GLO-30 publishes no tiles over the N41/E044 and N41/E045 cells — they are withheld from
the free instance for this border region. SRTM 1-arcsec is the stage brief's named
alternative at the same 30 m ground resolution, so the substitution costs nothing in
resolution, but it is a source difference worth recording.
