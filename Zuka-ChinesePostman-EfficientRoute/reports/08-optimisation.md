## 2. Where the saving comes from

Stage 4 gives each required arc to one day and Stage 5 solves that day as a closed circuit over its own zone, so credit was **per-day**: the car drove a street on day 3 while repositioning, earned nothing, and came back on day 7 to film it. Nothing about the camera changed here — it was always on. What changed is that a pass driven at filming speed now discharges the requirement **whenever it happens**.

|  | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Passes credited that the route already drove | 5,306 |
| Coverage harvested from them | 314.05 km |
| Time spent holding those passes to filming speed | 28.0 min |
| Cost per kilometre harvested | 5.3 s/km |
| Driving hours saved | 14.03 h |
| Days removed | 2 |
| Zone splits Stage 5 needed that this plan did not | 2 |
**The trade is extremely one-sided, and that is the finding.** Holding a pass to filming speed costs almost nothing on the streets where it matters: a residential arc is already driven near the cap, so the slowdown is seconds, while the revisit it replaces costs a return trip across the zone. On `capture_quality = "reduced"` arcs — motorway and trunk, exempt from the 40 km/h rule under SPEC §3.2 — the credit is free outright.

**Where the days go.** A day is removed not by shortening it but by emptying it: once earlier days have filmed enough of a later zone, that zone owes nothing and its day disappears. The rest of the saving is zones that no longer overrun their window, so Stage 5's split never has to happen.

## 5. Why the saving is real

A saving of this kind is easy to fake in three ways, so each is checked:

| Could be faked by | Invariant | Result |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Not actually filming the streets | **I1**, campaign-wide: every requirement discharged somewhere | 0 left undischarged |
| Filming them too fast to count | **I9**: every credited pass satisfies the Stage 6 rule | 0 failures |
| Never paying for the slowdown | **I10**: credited deadhead passes costed at capture time | holds |
| Running overlong days | **I4**: no day exceeds its window after slowing | 0 over |
**I9 is asserted against `coverage.qualifies` — the same function Stage 6 applies to a real GPS trace.** One implementation, two callers. If plan-time and run-time crediting were written separately they would drift, and the plan would bank coverage the rig does not deliver: the exact failure Stage 6 exists to catch, arriving a stage earlier and much harder to see.

**I1 changed shape and the old form was deleted, not kept alongside.** Asserting a street was filmed *on its assigned day* would now fail a correct plan — that is precisely the constraint this stage removes.
