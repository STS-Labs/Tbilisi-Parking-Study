Every one of these is a restriction that contradicts the one-way tagging of its own
members: the `from` way never ends at the via node, or the `to` way never leaves it.
Either the restriction or the `oneway` tag is wrong in OSM. They are logged with their
relation ids in `data/derived/01-restrictions.csv`, not silently dropped.


**Via-way restrictions (12) are honoured exactly, by cloning the via arc.**
A via-way constraint spans two manoeuvres, which a first-order edge-based graph cannot
express. The clone is reachable only from the restriction's `from` arc and loses the
forbidden continuation; the original keeps it for every other approach. Removing either
edge instead would have been legal but would have deleted drivable manoeuvres and
manufactured traps. Every L-node carries its physical `arc_id`, so Stage 3 groups coverage
by arc and never double-counts a clone.


## 3. U-turn edges


### Created by the §3.3 policy

| Reason created | Edges |
| --- | ---: |
| `dead_end_tip` | 18,100 |
| `width_permitted` | 3,458 |
| **Total created** | **21,558** |

Of those, **221** were then deleted again by a `no_u_turn` relation, leaving
**21,337** reverse-arc U-turns standing in L. The policy and the restrictions are
independent filters and both have to pass.


### Surviving in L, by kind


| Manoeuvre | Edges | Governed by the §3.3 U-turn policy? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reverse-arc U-turn (`a → reverse(a)`) | 21,337 | yes |
| Hairpin onto a *different* arc | 4,268 | no — an ordinary manoeuvre |

The turn-class table above counts 25,605 manoeuvres as `u_turn` by bearing, but only
21,337 of them are reverse-arc U-turns. The other 4,268 double back onto a **different**
arc: switchbacks on the hillside roads of Mtatsminda and Vake, and the turnaround loops that link
the two carriageways of a dual. Those are ordinary legal manoeuvres that merely look like
U-turns to a bearing test, and SPEC §3.3's policy does not govern them — it governs
`a → reverse(a)`. Conflating the two would make the policy appear to have created thousands
of edges it never touched.


SPEC §3.3 requires reverse-arc U-turns to be *absent*
where the manoeuvre is not physically possible, not merely penalised, so they are created
only at a dead-end tip or where the carriageway carries positive evidence of room:
`width ≥ 12.0 m` or `lanes ≥ 4`. SPEC leaves that threshold
**[CONFIRM]**; §1.1 fixes the direction, because inventing a manoeuvre that cannot be driven
breaks invariant U1 while withholding one only costs deadhead. No U-turn edge exists on
`motorway`, `motorway_link`, `trunk`, `trunk_link`.

## 7. Manual spot-check — 20 junctions against satellite imagery

Automated tests can only prove that L honours the restrictions OSM records. They cannot
catch a restriction that OSM does not have at all, which is why this table exists. Each
junction was read off Esri World Imagery at the coordinates given and compared with the
manoeuvres L actually permits.

**What this check can and cannot catch.** Overhead imagery shows *physical* constraints —
medians, splitter islands, kerb lines, dedicated turnaround loops, carriageway width. It
does not show *signage*, so a signed no-left-turn that OSM has not recorded is invisible to
it. A junction marked `confirmed` below means the manoeuvres L permits and bans are
consistent with the physical layout, not that OSM's restriction set is proven complete.
Closing that gap needs street-level imagery or a survey, and is worth doing before Stage 5
commits a driver to a route.

Junctions were chosen deterministically: the 12 carrying a via-way relation first, since
those are the ones a naive implementation gets wrong, then the busiest restricted junctions
by count, with the landmark categories the stage brief names (Heroes' Square, riverside
embankment, central couplets) snapped to the nearest restricted junction. Names below are
the street names actually found at each node, not the landmark that selected it.

## 8. Where this stage departs from SPEC, and why

### SPEC §3.3: "Motorway and trunk nodes … are enterable only via ramp arcs"

That clause is written for grade-separated roads. In Tbilisi, `highway=trunk` is an
at-grade arterial class — Stage 0 measured 446 two-way trunk segments with ordinary
junctions. Applying the clause to trunk deletes real, drivable manoeuvres. Measured
both ways:

| Mainline classes | Manoeuvres in L | SCC share of required km | Required km lost | Gate ≥ 99% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (none) | 287,856 | 99.9% | 5.9 | PASS |
| `motorway` | 287,850 | 99.9% | 6.7 | PASS |
| `motorway`, `trunk` | 286,900 | 96.5% | 195.0 | **FAIL** |
**Decision: the rule is applied to `motorway` only**, which is what the current graph
carries (`motorway`).
Extending it to trunk as the spec's wording implies fails the Stage 1 gate outright.
This is flagged rather than resolved silently: the wording assumes a road class that
Tbilisi's data does not match, and SPEC should be amended to say *grade-separated*
mainlines rather than naming trunk. Note also that OSM already encodes grade separation
topologically — a bridge does not share a node with the road beneath it — so for
correctly mapped junctions the rule is close to a no-op and mostly removes connections
the data says are real.

### SPEC §3.3 edge cost vs SPEC §11 stage order

SPEC §3.3 defines an edge cost as *traversal time of `b` + turn penalty*, but SPEC §11
places the time model in Stage 2. Rather than resolve that silently in either direction,
L carries the two components **separately**: the turn penalty is a real Stage 1 product,
and traversal time comes from an explicitly provisional speed model — posted `maxspeed`
where tagged, a free-flow-by-class table otherwise, capped at the 40 km/h capture speed
except on the reduced-quality motorway and trunk arcs of §3.2. No congestion, no gradient,
no idle allowance. Stage 2 replaces one function and does not rebuild the graph.
