## 1. The sizing model against the solver

Stage 4 sizes a day as `capture x overhead + legs + break`, so `overhead` is
the driving *inside* the zone per hour of capture — the depot legs are a
separate term beside it. Measured over the 47 days solved here:

| Statistic | Intra-zone overhead |
| --- | ---: |
| min | 1.781 |
| p50 | 2.102 |
| p75 | 2.203 |
| p90 | 2.362 |
| p95 | 2.775 |
| max | 3.155 |

**Why the depot legs must stay out of it.** `route_h` contains both legs, so
`route_h / capture_h` reads about a leg-pair-per-capture-hour higher than the
figure above. Feeding *that* to a model which then adds the legs again charges
them twice and inflates every zone's predicted day — which is exactly what
`ZONE_OVERHEAD_MEASURED = 2.55` did, and what Stage 4c corrected.

**The commute credit.** SPEC §7.1's first mitigation is real and Stage 4 once
modelled it — but *Stage 5 does not implement it*. Each day is solved as a
closed circuit over its own zone; its legs do pass through other zones, but at
deadhead speed, capturing nothing. **A plan may not bank a saving its solver
does not deliver**, so the credit stays withdrawn
(`CAPTURE_ON_COMMUTE_IN_SIZING = False`) and `COMMUTE_ALLOWANCE` with it.
Implementing it properly — solving days in sequence and letting each day's legs
capture what later days still owe — is worth about 12 h and belongs with Stage
6, which already tracks coverage across days.

**4 zones still would not fit after
re-sizing and had to be split.** Shrinking every zone to accommodate one awkward
one costs a day per zone; splitting the offender costs exactly one day, so the
repair is local. Each split half was re-solved and re-checked.

| Zone split | Became |

| 4 | 4 + 43 |
| 2 | 2 + 44 |

| 33 | 33 + 46 |

SPEC §4's KPI warning applies: **total driving hours is the KPI**, and the ratio is a
diagnostic the coverage rules deliberately inflate.

### The optimality gap

Each day's gap is measured against a rigorous floor — the zone's required capture
time plus the cheapest legal way to leave each required traversal. Both terms are
unavoidable and disjoint, so the floor is valid; it accounts for **no repositioning
at all**, which is why the gaps look large. It is a bound, not a target.

Campaign gap against that floor: **101.5%**
(321.9 h against a floor of 159.8 h).

## 3. Against the Stage 3 bracket (I5)

|  | Hours |
| --- | ---: |
| Stage 3 lower bound (LP relaxation) | 178.6 |
| Stage 3 upper bound (one closed tour) | 272.0 |
| **Stage 5, 47 day routes** | **321.9** |

Stage 5 drives 49.9 h more than Stage 3's single
tour, and that is expected rather than a regression. Stage 3 solved one closed tour of the whole scope with no depot legs and no day boundaries. A 47-day plan drives to and from the depot 47 times, so it sits above the upper end by construction.

**So I5 as written does not apply to a day plan.** SPEC §8 I5 asks that total driving
lie within the Stage 3 bracket; the bracket was computed for a *different problem* —
one tour, no depot, no shift limit. A 48-day plan is strictly more constrained and
must cost more. The comparison worth making is the one above: the day plan against
the tour, with the difference attributed to the depot returns the tour never made.
That difference is 49.9 h over 47 days —
about 64 minutes a day of
getting to work and home again, which is the price of having a depot on the western
edge of the city (SPEC §7.1).

## 4. The turn account, settled

Stage 2 could only give a range — 91.2 to 145.0 h — because a turn penalty cannot be
summed without knowing which manoeuvres get made, and it swung the campaign by 7.3
days. Stage 3 counted a real tour. Stage 5 counts real routes.

| Source | Turn hours | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stage 2 floor — cheapest legal exit | 91.2 | estimate, variant A |
| Stage 2 estimate — mean legal exit | 145.0 | estimate, variant A |
| Stage 3 tour, variant C | 51.0 | one closed tour, counted exactly |
| **Stage 5, 47 day routes** | **56.6** | counted exactly |

The count is now exact: 113,013 traversals, each followed by one
manoeuvre whose class is read off `L(G)`. Stage 2's numbers were for variant A and
are not directly comparable in magnitude, but the shape of its finding holds — turn
time is 17.6% of all driving, which is
not a rounding term.

**What is settled is the count, not the coefficients.** The per-turn seconds — left 8 s, right 3 s, straight 0 s, u_turn 20 s —
are SPEC §3.3's own approximations, still marked **[CONFIRM values]** in the spec and
never measured against a real vehicle. Every turn figure in this project scales
linearly with them. A stopwatch at twenty junctions would retire the largest
remaining unvalidated assumption in the time model, and Stage 6's traces will carry
the data to do it properly.

## 5. Orientation — the untouched lever

SPEC §4 Stage D lists an orientation-flip local search that has never been run.
Stage 3 showed why it matters: its routing was within 1% of optimal *for a fixed
orientation*, and the imbalance heuristic that chose that orientation removed only
6.5%
of junction imbalance. If savings remain anywhere, they are here.

**Not run, and the reason is a cost, not an oversight.** Evaluating a single
orientation flip means re-solving that zone's min-cost flow — about 11 seconds.
A zone holds several hundred narrow pairs and there are 48 zones, so one pass of
a true flip search is on the order of 90 hours of compute. It needs an
incremental evaluation (flip the pair, repair the flow locally rather than
re-solving it) before it is affordable, and that is a piece of work in its own
right.

## 6. What the driver gets

| Artifact | Count | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reports/05-routes/day-NN.gpx` | 47 | one track per day, closed, depot to depot |
| `reports/05-routes/day-NN.md` | 47 | turn-by-turn sheet, straight runs folded into one line |
| `data/derived/05-routes.gpkg` | 113,013 | every manoeuvre, in order, with its mode and lane instruction |

**Lane instructions (invariant I8).** 5,329 arc-days need two same-direction
passes, and 7,892 traversals carry an explicit lane group. SPEC §2.3: without
it the driver repeats the same lane and the second pass is wasted footage. Georgia
drives on the right, so pass 1 takes the kerb side and pass 2 the offside.

The sheets fold consecutive straight-ahead steps along the same named street into a
single instruction. A sheet with fifteen hundred rows saying "continue" is not a
usable document, and the driver needs an instruction only where something changes.
Capture rows carry the 40 km/h limit; deadhead rows say so explicitly, because a
driver who captures on a repositioning leg is wasting the day's daylight.
