# Stage 8 across every campaign — does the saving generalise?

Generated by `tools/stage8_summary.py` from each campaign's
`data/derived/08-sequential.json`. Nothing is recomputed here; every number is
the one that campaign's own Stage 8 gate asserted.


## The answer: yes, on every campaign, in both days and hours


| Campaign | | Before | After | Δ days | Δ hours | Credited | Cost of credit |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Tbilisi** | whole city, variant C | 47 d, 321.9 h | 45 d, 307.9 h | **-2** | **-14.0 h** (-4.4%) | 314 km | 28 min |
| **Zone B — variant C** | 24 km² zone, service excluded | 7 d, 35.5 h | 5 d, 33.2 h | **-2** | **-2.3 h** (-6.4%) | 25 km | 3 min |
| **Run 1 — OSM full** | same zone, service included | 21 d, 100.8 h | 19 d, 96.5 h | **-2** | **-4.4 h** (-4.3%) | 85 km | 11 min |
| **Run 2 — customer net** | same zone, customer's street list | 13 d, 71.4 h | 12 d, 67.8 h | **-1** | **-3.6 h** (-5.1%) | 62 km | 10 min |

**It generalises.** Four campaigns spanning 7 to 47 days, and required sets
from 24.9% to 39.6% of the network that connects them, all save between 4.3% and
6.4% of driving and all lose at least one day. Nothing here is specific to one
geometry.


**And every campaign saves it the same way.** The credit is bought at a few
seconds per kilometre — the slowdown from repositioning speed to filming speed on
streets the car was already driving — and repaid by a revisit that never has to
happen. Tbilisi harvests 314 km of coverage for 28 minutes of holding to 40 km/h.


## What each tier bought


| Campaign | Tier 0 | Tier 1 — passive credit | Tier 2 — order search |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Tbilisi | 47 d, 321.9 h | 47 d, 310.5 h | 45 d, 307.9 h |
| Zone B — variant C | 7 d, 35.5 h | 6 d, 33.3 h | 5 d, 33.2 h |
| Run 1 — OSM full | 21 d, 100.8 h | 20 d, 96.8 h | 19 d, 96.5 h |
| Run 2 — customer net | 13 d, 71.4 h | 12 d, 67.9 h | 12 d, 67.8 h |

**Tier 1 takes 91% of the hours; tier 2 takes the days.** Passive
crediting — banking passes the route already drives — is where almost all of the
driving time goes. Changing *which day runs first* then removed a further day on
3 of 4 campaigns for almost no additional hours, because
credit lands on whichever days go early and Stage 4's far-zones-first sequence was
chosen to shorten depot legs by a rule that predates crediting entirely.


## Which order won, and what that says


| Campaign | Best order |
| --- | --- |
| Tbilisi | seeded shuffle 20260828 |
| Zone B — variant C | largest-zone-first |
| Run 1 — OSM full | smallest-zone-first |
| Run 2 — customer net | smallest-zone-first |

**3 different orders win across 4 campaigns, and no rule
predicts which.** One campaign wants its largest zone first, two want their
smallest, and the city wants an order no principle proposed at all. The obvious
intuition — that the biggest zone should lead, because it has the most to give
away — holds on exactly one of the four.

That is the honest reading, and more useful than a rule would have been:
**the gain is real and its location is not predictable**, which is precisely
the case for the Large Neighbourhood Search of §2. Four hunches plus four
shuffles is a poor instrument for finding it; a search that moves individual
days between assignments is the right one, and this says it would have
something to find.


## Where the days come from


A day disappears in one of two ways, and neither is 'the day got shorter'.
Either a zone ends up owing nothing at all, because earlier days filmed it in
passing, or a zone that Stage 5 had to **split** for overrunning its window now
fits in one. The second is the more common, and it is why the saving in days is
larger than the saving in hours would suggest.
